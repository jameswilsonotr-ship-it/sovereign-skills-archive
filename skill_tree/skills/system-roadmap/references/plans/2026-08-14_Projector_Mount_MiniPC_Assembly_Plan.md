## 3. Complete Assembly Plan (PC-side)

### Design Concept — Isolation Sandwich + Quick-Swap Modules
The core idea is a **center-bolt (or dual-bolt) sandwich** that turns each mini-PC into a self-contained, vibration-isolated module:

1. Rubber grommet against the PC chassis (both sides of the mounting hole).  
2. 1/4" × 1-1/4" fender washer on the outside of each grommet (load spread).  
3. 1/4"-20 × 1" button-head screw through the entire stack.  
4. Wing nut for tool-free (or nearly tool-free) attachment to an Alrhipvm plate or custom adapter.

This stack protects the thin HP chassis, damps road vibration, and allows the entire PC + plate assembly to be lifted off in seconds.

### Primary Use Case (Truck / Projector Mount)
1. Attach one Alrhipvm plate (or a flat adapter plate) to the adjustable spider arms of the Inland PRB-20-01M using the 1/4"-20 hardware.  
2. For each mini-PC:  
   - Place rubber grommet on both sides of the chosen chassis hole (center or one of the 100 × 100 holes).  
   - Sandwich with fender washers.  
   - Pass 1/4"-20 × 1" bolt through the stack.  
   - Secure with wing nut.  
3. Units hang upright or can be locked at desired angle via the projector mount’s tilt/swivel.  
4. Vibration is isolated by the rubber layers; load is distributed by the fender washers. Total mass of two G9s + K15 is trivial relative to the 77 lb capacity of the PRB-20-01M.

### Expansion / Shore-Side Strategy
- Pre-mount the extra Alrhipvm VESA plates at fixed locations (home wall, workbench, second vehicle, parents’ house, etc.).  
- The mini-PC + grommet + washer + bolt stack becomes a quick-swap module.  
- Unscrew wing nuts at the truck mount → lift the whole assembly off → walk it to the shore-side plate → re-bolt.  
- Reverse for return to truck.  
- Extra VESA mounts give multiple secure docking stations without ever touching the projector mount itself.  
- Symmetry preference: label left/right G9 modules for mirrored pairing when desired.

### Why this configuration wins
- Weight is trivial (~3.1 lb per G9 + <2 lb for K15).  
- Center-bolt or dual-bolt through existing holes is structurally fine.  
- Rubber on both sides + fender washers = isolation + no chassis damage.  
- Wing nuts = rapid, nearly tool-free transfer.  
- Surplus Alrhipvm plates = future-proof expansion and clean shore-side parking.  
- Avoids forcing the heavy spider arms of the PRB-20-01M directly onto thin mini-PC shells (risk of crushing or stripping threads).

## 4. Vehicle-End Mounting Options (Cab / Sleeper)

Context: Riverside Transport company driver, Kansas City terminal, ~68 ¢/mi, 2 weeks out / 3 days home, primarily drop-and-hook. Fleet is Freightliner Cascadia / International LT class of sleeper. Prefer reversible or low-damage solutions because it is a company truck.

### A. The Six Options from the Recap Email (ranked for Cascadia/LT reality)

1. **Roll Cage or Exo-Skeleton Tube Clamping**  
   Heavy-duty billet aluminum roll-bar clamps or interlocking tube couplers matched to bar diameter (1.5–2"). Bolt the Inland ceiling plate to the clamp tabs with 1/4"-20 hardware.  
   *Truck filter*: Rare on stock Cascadia/LT sleepers. Only useful if the unit already has aftermarket cage or overhead bars. Otherwise low priority.

2. **Headliner Crossmember Rivnut Anchoring**  
   Locate structural roof bows behind the headliner. Install steel Rivnuts (threaded inserts) with a setting tool, then bolt the projector plate flush.  
   *Truck filter*: Realistic and strong. Document thoroughly for company equipment. Permanent but clean if done carefully.

3. **Unistrut / Superstrut Roof Rail Grid**  
   Run parallel 1-5/8" Unistrut channels across the ceiling, anchored to side pillars or roof ribs. Use channel spring nuts to position the mount anywhere along the rails.  
   *Truck filter*: More van/overland style. Possible but heavy and space-hungry in a sleeper. Lower priority unless already running channel.

4. **Through-Roof Bolting with Exterior Backing Plates**  
   Pass-through bolts with marine-grade stainless backing plates (or large fender washers) on the exterior roof, sealed with butyl or marine silicone.  
   *Truck filter*: Highest strength, highest warranty/lease risk on a company truck. Only with written permission or ownership.

5. **Heavy-Duty Partition / Bulkhead Mounting**  
   Mount the base plate vertically to a solid sleeper/cab divider using structural L-brackets, then tilt the projector body 90° so it aims aft.  
   *Truck filter*: Excellent if a solid partition exists. Keeps mass lower and reduces pure vertical bounce. High practicality for drop-and-hook work.

6. **Seat Track / Floor-Based Pedestal Anchor**  
   Unbolt passenger (or driver) seat track bolts, slide a heavy steel base plate under the track, re-torque factory bolts. Run a rigid vertical pedestal up to the projector interface.  
   *Truck filter*: Highest-probability winner for leased company trucks. 100 % reversible, zero permanent damage, uses the strongest existing anchors in the chassis. Same principle as police laptop mounts.

### B. Five Additional RAM-Mount-Specific Options (original)

7. **RAM Tough-Ball + Double-Socket Arm on Seat Rail**  
   RAM 1.5" or 2.25" ball mounted to a seat-rail plate. Double-socket arm reaches up to a second ball on the projector base plate. Allows full articulation and collapses flat against the seat back when not in use.

8. **RAM X-Grip or Universal Cradle Adapter Plate**  
   Bolt a flat aluminum plate to the projector base, then use a large RAM X-Grip or cradle that clamps the plate. The grip itself mounts to any existing RAM ball already in the truck (common for phones/tablets).

9. **RAM Tele-Pole / Vertical Extension from Floor Track**  
   RAM vertical tele-pole or rigid post that rises from a seat-track base and terminates in a ball. Projector mount hangs from the top ball. Height-adjustable and fully removable.

10. **RAM Yoke / U-Bolt Clamp on Overhead Handrail or Bunk Frame**  
    If the sleeper has any tubular overhead rail or bunk support tube, use a RAM yoke or U-bolt clamp to hang a short arm that carries the projector plate. Completely reversible.

11. **RAM Dual-Ball “Floating” Isolation Link**  
    Two short RAM arms in series with a rubber-isolated intermediate plate. Provides an extra layer of vibration isolation between the vehicle structure and the projector mount while still allowing articulation.

### C. Five Additional Alternative Options (original, non-RAM)

12. **Bunk-Rail or Overhead Storage Bin Clamp System**  
    Custom or commercial C-clamps / beam clamps that grip the existing metal rails under the upper bunk or the edge of the overhead storage bins. Projector plate bolts to a short horizontal bar spanning two clamps.

13. **Refrigerator Cabinet Side-Mount with Heavy L-Brackets**  
    Many Cascadia/LT sleepers have a refrigerator cabinet against the wall. Structural L-brackets bolted into the cabinet side (or into solid blocking behind it) create a vertical mounting face for the projector base. Low and stable.

14. **Passenger-Seat Headrest Post Clamp**  
    Specialized headrest-post clamps (common in mobile-office setups) that lock onto the two vertical headrest posts. A horizontal bar between the clamps carries the projector plate. Fully removable in seconds.

15. **Sleeper Floor “T-Track” or Cargo-Track Adaptation**  
    If the truck has any factory or aftermarket cargo track in the sleeper floor, use sliding T-nuts or cargo anchors as the base for a short vertical post. Extremely strong and completely reversible.

16. **Magnetic Base + Safety Lanyard (temporary only)**  
    High-force neodymium magnetic base on a clean steel surface (e.g., refrigerator side or metal bulkhead) with a secondary safety lanyard or ratchet strap as backup. Only for temporary / low-speed use; not primary highway solution.

## 5. User Ideas (from this conversation) — fleshed out with two original variations each

**User Idea A — Long bar-clamp over wood / 4×4 on the rear desk surface over the refrigerator**

The long orange-handled clamp (or similar bar/pipe clamp) spans a piece of wood or 4×4 that sits on the little desk surface above the refrigerator. The projector mount (or the Alrhipvm plate stack) is clamped to that wood so the entire assembly is held down by the clamp force rather than by drilling.

- **Variation A1 (original)**: Replace the wood with a short length of Unistrut or aluminum angle iron that has pre-drilled holes matching the projector base plate. The bar clamp still holds the Unistrut down, but the projector plate now bolts solidly into the Unistrut instead of relying only on clamp friction. Adds rigidity without changing the core “clamp-over-desk” concept.  
- **Variation A2 (original)**: Use two shorter clamps, one at each end of a wider plywood or phenolic board that spans the full depth of the desk surface. The board becomes a removable “docking tray.” Projector mount (or quick-swap Alrhipvm modules) bolts to the tray; the whole tray lifts out when the clamps are released. Cleaner cable management and easier packing.

**User Idea B — Articulated projector mount that collapses flat or lays on the bunk mattress**

The PRB-20-01M already has tilt, swivel, and rotation. The design goal is to fold the entire arm assembly so it can either (a) collapse against a wall or bulkhead or (b) be laid flat on the mattress when the truck is moving hard.

- **Variation B1 (original)**: Add a simple hinge plate or RAM dual-ball intermediate link between the vehicle anchor and the projector base. This creates a second fold point so the whole assembly can be rotated down and strapped flat against the bunk platform or the side of the refrigerator cabinet.  
- **Variation B2 (original)**: Keep the projector mount fully articulated for daytime use, but design a dedicated “travel cradle” — a shallow padded tray that lives under the bunk or in a storage bin. When preparing to roll, the entire spider + PC stack is lifted off its vehicle anchor and dropped into the cradle, then the cradle is secured with the existing bunk straps or a couple of ratchet straps. Zero modification to the mount itself.

**User Idea C — Seatbelt or ratchet straps on the passenger seat**

Use the factory passenger seatbelt (or a pair of ratchet straps) to secure the collapsed projector mount / PC cluster against the passenger seat back or base while driving.

- **Variation C1 (original)**: Fabricate (or buy) a simple fabric “saddlebag” or padded sleeve that the entire collapsed assembly slides into. The sleeve has seatbelt loops or D-rings so the factory belt passes through and cinches it against the seat. Protects finishes and keeps the mass centered.  
- **Variation C2 (original)**: Mount a short vertical post or plate permanently (or semi-permanently) to the passenger seat base via the seat-track bolts (same principle as option 6). The projector assembly then has a matching quick-release plate that drops onto that post and is retained by a single pin or the seatbelt itself. Combines the strength of the seat track with the convenience of the belt as a secondary retainer.

## 6. Weight & Safety Notes

- Combined weight of 2× G9 + K15 ≈ 8–9 lb. Negligible relative to any of the structural options above.  
- The 77 lb capacity of the PRB-20-01M is more than enough even with the spider arms fully extended.  
- Primary risk is vibration fatigue and accidental release, not static load. The rubber-grommet sandwich + wing-nut design addresses both.  
- Always use a secondary safety tether (light cable or ratchet strap) on any hanging assembly until the preferred vehicle anchor is proven over a few thousand miles.  
- Company-truck rule of thumb: prefer reversible solutions (seat track, clamps, straps, temporary cradles) unless written approval for Rivnuts or through-bolts is obtained.
